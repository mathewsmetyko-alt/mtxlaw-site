Understanding Texas Personal Injury Law

Statute of Limitations

In Texas, you generally have two years from the date of injury to file a personal injury lawsuit. Missing this deadline almost always means losing your right to recover — regardless of how strong your case is. Do not wait.

Modified Comparative Fault

Texas follows a modified comparative fault rule. If you are found to be 51% or more at fault for your own injury, you cannot recover. If you are 50% or less at fault, your recovery is reduced proportionally. Insurance companies routinely try to inflate your percentage of fault — having an experienced attorney in your corner is critical.

Damages You May Be Entitled To

  • Past and future medical expenses
  • Lost wages and loss of earning capacity
  • Pain and suffering
  • Mental anguish
  • Physical impairment and disfigurement
  • Punitive damages (in cases of gross negligence)

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to hire a personal injury attorney?

Nothing upfront. Personal injury cases are handled on contingency — we only get paid if you win. Our fee comes as a percentage of your recovery. If we don't recover for you, you owe nothing.

The insurance company already made me an offer. Should I take it?

Almost certainly not — at least not without having an attorney review it first. Early offers are almost always far below the value of your claim. Once you accept a settlement, you typically cannot go back for more, even if your injuries turn out to be worse than expected.

How long will my case take?

It depends on the complexity of your injuries, the willingness of the insurance company to negotiate fairly, and whether the case needs to go to trial. Simple cases may resolve in months. Serious injury cases may take a year or more. We keep you informed throughout.

What should I do immediately after an accident?

Seek medical attention first — even if you feel fine, some injuries don't present symptoms immediately. Document everything: photos, witness information, the other driver's insurance. Don't give recorded statements to insurance adjusters. Call us before you say anything.
